# UBS Your Investor Profile (January 2025) - condensed guide

## Investor Profile (page 2)
The Investor Profile is defined in four stages. (1) Financial situation gives a holistic view of risk ability, i.e. the capacity to take financial risk without putting the current lifestyle or future plans at risk. (2) Investment objectives show financial goals, sustainability preference and how much risk the client is willing to take to achieve the target return, i.e. risk tolerance. (3) The assessment compares risk tolerance with risk ability so recommended products meet the objectives and the ability to bear losses. (4) Knowledge and experience is an additional factor for informed decisions. The Investor Profile must be current, accurate and complete and is reviewed regularly.

## Financial situation (page 3)
Free assets are the assets not tied to liabilities or planned expenses and available at any time; they represent the ability to absorb losses (loss capacity). Free asset ratio = free assets / net liquid assets. Example: liquid assets 5,000,000 less current liabilities 1,000,000 = net liquid assets 4,000,000; less planned expenses 400,000 = free assets 3,600,000; free asset ratio 90%. If 2,000,000 is held with the bank, loss capacity there = 2,000,000 x 90% = 1,800,000.

## Investment objectives (page 4)
The purpose of the investments (reserves for retirement, a planned larger expenditure, growing or preserving wealth) influences risk tolerance and strategy. With several goals it can be sensible to set up different portfolios, each with its own horizon. Investment horizon is the period the client wants to stay invested. Under identical conditions a longer horizon allows riskier investments (larger fluctuations, longer recovery); a shorter horizon calls for low-risk investments. Risk tolerance is set per objective or portfolio on six model strategies from A (very low risk tolerance) to F (high risk tolerance). Sustainability preference: no preference, low, or high, plus optional Principal Adverse Impacts exclusions.

## Model investment strategies (pages 5-7)
Six model investment strategies were developed by the Chief Investment Office, each with a typical risk/return profile: A Fixed Income, B Income, C Yield, D Balanced, E Growth, F Equities. Approximate expected annual return / volatility: A 2% / 2.5%, B 3% / 4.5%, C 3.5% / 7%, D 4.5% / 9%, E 5% / 11.5%, F 6% / 14%. Maximum historical drawdown (approximate): A -15%, B -15%, C -25%, D -35%, E -45%, F -50%. Longest period of a historical drawdown: A 4 years, B 4 years, C 4 years, D 6 years, E 6 years, F 7 years. Historical stress tests (worst event per strategy): A -10% (interest rate hikes, ongoing), B -13% (financial crisis, 1y5m under water), C -22% (financial crisis, 1y11m), D -29% (financial crisis, 2y8m), E -37% (financial crisis, 3y0m), F -43% (financial crisis, 4y9m). As a basic rule the higher the risk tolerance the higher the expected return, and the higher the fluctuations and recovery time.
Strategic asset allocation (illustrative): A Fixed Income = Bonds 95%, Liquidity 5%. B Income = Bonds 72%, Equities 13%, Alternative Investments 7%, Liquidity 5%, Other 3%. C Yield = Bonds 51%, Equities 29%, Alternative Investments 11%, Liquidity 5%, Other 4%. D Balanced = Bonds 33%, Equities 47%, Alternative Investments 11%, Liquidity 5%, Other 4%. E Growth = Bonds 15%, Equities 70%, Alternative Investments 7%, Liquidity 5%, Other 3%. F Equities = Equities 95%, Liquidity 5%. Strategic asset allocation is the most important long-term driver of performance; the higher the equity component, the higher the return potential and the possible fluctuation in value.

## Assessment of the Investor Profile (page 8)
Risk budget = investment amount x maximum historical drawdown of the chosen strategy. Example: 1,000,000 in C-Moderate (25% drawdown) = risk budget 250,000. Total risk budget is the sum across portfolios (conservative: all assets assumed to fall together). Lombard loans raise investment risk and the risk budget. Assessment result: as long as the risk budget is below the loss capacity, the risk taken is in line with the financial situation. Investment horizon: the horizon of each portfolio must be at least the longest historical drawdown of its strategy so there is time to recover.

## Sustainability preference (pages 9-10)
Preferences can be set under EU regulatory standards (SFDR, EU Taxonomy, Principal Adverse Impacts) and/or the UBS methodology: no preference, low, or high, with an optional minimum proportion invested sustainably. The proportion of sustainable investments is compared with the selected preference in the assessment.

## Knowledge and experience (pages 11-12)
General market experience, experience in product categories, and knowledge per product category are assessed. Target market and MiFID II client classification (retail or professional) apply.

## Next steps (page 13)
Align the portfolio with the Investor Profile, stay disciplined, diversify broadly, and review the profile regularly.

## Glossary highlights (pages 14-15)
Free assets: total liquid assets less liabilities and expenses planned for the next five years and any reserves. Investment horizon: minimum expected length of time a client would like to stay invested in a portfolio. Loss capacity / risk ability: ability to expose investments to risk without impairing standard of living or suffering liquidity problems. Risk tolerance: the degree of risk a client is prepared to accept to achieve an expected return, scale A to F. Maximum historical drawdown: maximum loss of a model strategy derived from history if bought and sold at the worst time.
